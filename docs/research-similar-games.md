# Learning from the neighbours: games like Oakridge Crawlspace

Phase 8 research, written 2026-11-17. Nothing here is built yet. Rohan picks what gets adopted, after
the game is done.

**Rohan's ask:** "Go online and search up games similar to these, fears to fathom and see what
seperates us from them and make it not a carbon copy but take the positives and negatives".

## The short version
- **The genre's biggest complaint isn't about scares. It's about losing progress.** Players hate
  replaying 10 to 20 minutes after a death, a crash or a bug. We have the same problem in Act 4.
- **The best-loved moments are ordinary ones:** the routine, the pet, silly freedom with everyday
  objects, and slow small wrongness. Scares that only start at the end get punished.
- **Trial-and-error deaths, with a jump scare each time, make people quit.** A fail needs a clear reason
  and a fair retry.
- **We are already ahead in the places critics say this genre is thin:** a real villain, choices that
  change the ending, a game that learns your habits, and a pet that comforts you.
- **The "true story" claim is the series' weak spot.** We can be honest instead: phrogging is real,
  Oakridge is not.

## How this was researched
- **Steam reviews:** for every game below, I read the "most helpful" English reviews, both positive
  and negative, on 2026-11-17. Reviews change over time, so the links show today's list, not a frozen
  copy.
- **In the tables:** "Steam +" links to the positive reviews and "Steam −" to the negative ones. Ratings
  and prices come from each game's Steam store page on the same day.
- **Also used:** critic reviews, a developer interview, and news or wiki pages, all linked.
- **Not used:** Reddit blocks the tools I have, so there are no Reddit threads here. itch.io comments
  and Steam reviews stand in for them.
- **Paraphrased:** reviews are put into my own words. No long quotes.

## 1. Games surveyed

### Fears to Fathom (Rayll Studios): the main comparison
- **What it is:** an anthology of short first-person horror episodes.
  - Each one is an ordinary situation (home alone, a motel, house-sitting) that slowly goes wrong.
  - Each is told as if by the person who lived through it, with a VHS look, phone texts, and the
    player's microphone used while hiding.
- **Who made it:** one young developer. He collected 132 horror stories from fans and said people
  relating to it is why it took off ([Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/input/gaming/mukul-negi-rayll-fears-to-fathom-home-alone-norwood-hitchhike)).
- **How it spread:** YouTubers.
  - The developer credited Markiplier's playthrough for a boost ([itch.io](https://itch.io/post/808611)).
  - One Home Alone video passed 21 million views ([Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/subcultures/fears-to-fathom)).
- **Is it true?** Destructoid concludes the episodes aren't real stories, only realistic ones
  ([Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/are-fears-to-fathom-stories-real-story-and-episodes-explained/)).
  The developer himself admits he can't always tell which submissions are true ([Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/input/gaming/mukul-negi-rayll-fears-to-fathom-home-alone-norwood-hitchhike)).

## 4. What separates Oakridge from them

### Where we are already different, or ahead
- **Wes is a real character, not a faceless threat.**
  - Critics call this genre thin on twists and depth. One player found Woodbury's villain annoying
    rather than scary ([Steam +](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2961530/positivereviews/?browsefilter=toprated&filterLanguage=english)),
    and others found Dread Neighbor's killer predictable ([Steam −](https://steamcommunity.com/app/4111260/negativereviews/?browsefilter=toprated&filterLanguage=english)).
  - Wes has a history (the growth marks, the 1999 poster, the shed boxes), a body language you can read
    (the head tilt, three knocks, the unfolding), a voice, and a lullaby.
  - **Ours:** `docs/story-v2.md` ("The character").
- **The house learns you, and tells you.**
  - Hello Neighbor sold an AI that learns your habits, and critics say it didn't deliver.
  - Our habit gates (the breaker, the faltering light, the black TV) work through `js/engine/habits.js`,
    and the notebook in the crawl shows you that he watched.
  - Nobody in this survey reads your own night back to you.
- **The pet cam and the flash camera are tools, not decoration.**
  - Carson House's security cameras were the only system one critic liked, in a review that disliked
    the rest
    ([GameGrin](https://www.gamegrin.com/reviews/fears-to-fathom-carson-house-review/)).
  - Ours do more: alerts, saved clips, a crude tracker in Act 4, and evidence for the ending.
- **Day 2 in daylight.**
  - Most of these games are one night, or several near-identical nights (The Convenience Store).
  - Our day is a real rest with lore you can actually read, people to talk to (the deputy, the
    cashier), and a store trip.
- **Choices change the ending.** F2F's endings hinge on one obvious choice. Ours come from evidence:
  three tiers plus the bad, gag and deputy variants, plus achievements and chapter select.
- **Cooper.** Pets are loved across the genre. Cooper is comfort, a warning system (wag versus growl)
  and a guide in Act 4, and he is never hurt.
- **Jump scares are capped at 3, each followed by relief.** This is the opposite of the complaints
  about MADiSON's and Woodbury's spam.
- **Wes searches; he doesn't chase.** The Director pulls him back after peaks. This answers the
  burn-out players describe in Stay Out of the House, where the killer never stops hunting.
- **Free, offline, and one file.** There are no price complaints to worry about, and `node
  tools/bundle.js` makes a single HTML file.
- **The basics others miss are already in:**
  - pause and settings (sensitivity, volume, VHS strength, FOV, subtitles, head bob);
  - a to-do list that always says what's next;
  - `OC.debug.sweep()`, which catches soft-locks before players do.
- **Built-in humor:** Dave's notes and puns, the singing fish, Flappy Cooper, the Tupperware gag. This is
  the same kind of "streamer moment" that spread F2F, but written on purpose.

### Where we are behind
- **Act 4 restarts from the top on any fail.** `js/acts/act4.js:273` reports `'retry'`, and
  `js/main.js:193` runs `startAct(id)`.
  - So failing the 60-second hold at the car means replaying the bathroom, the whole search and the
    chain door: about 10 minutes.
  - That is exactly the genre's number-one complaint.
  - Act 3 (about 6 minutes) has the same shape.
- **No brightness setting.** `js/engine/settings.js:11` has sensitivity, volume, VHS, FOV, subtitle and
  bob, but no brightness. Our nights, the crawl and the storm are dark.
- **Little play with objects.** Only the tennis ball can be thrown (`js/decor.js:116`). F2F's most-shared
  moments come from messing with ordinary objects.
- **A repeated fail repeats a jump scare.** The closet burst (jump scare 2) plays on every failed bluff,
  (`js/acts/act3.js:305` to `:319`: every fail bursts the closet, then restarts Act 3), so a
  struggling player sees it again and again. Scratch Creek's players call that the worst sin.
- **No voice acting; everything is text.** In the genre this gets mixed reviews: F2F's narration was
  called immature by one critic ([Indie Hive](https://indie-hive.com/fears-to-fathom-ironbark-lookout/amp/))
  and praised by another ([Mirtheon](https://mirtheon.pages.dev/posts/the-best-fears-to-fathom-episodes)).
  It's a small gap.
- **No microphone option.** F2F uses the mic while hiding. It's memorable, but it misfires (section 3).
- **The look.** Ours is code-built and low-poly, against F2F's near-photo-real VHS. The genre clearly
  accepts a retro look (Puppet Combo), so we lean into it rather than chase realism.
- **No stranger has played it.** These games are judged by first-time players and streamers. Only
  Rohan can say whether ours scares, and our checks can't.

## 5. Proposals

Every proposal keeps the story rules:
- Cooper is never hurt.
- There are no weapons.
- There are at most 3 jump scares, each with relief.
- Every new line is hand-written.
- It runs offline from `file://`.

Costs: **S** is an afternoon, **M** is a day or two, **L** is a phase of its own.

### P1. Checkpoints inside The Search (and The Call)
- **What:**
  - Save a checkpoint at the beats of Act 4: after the bathroom, at the front door, and at the car.
  - A fail goes back to the last one, not to the bathroom.
  - Act 3 gets one after the closet-tap choice.
- **Evidence:** losing progress is the top complaint across F2F, Chilla's Art and Night Delivery
  (section 3, row 1).
- **Touches:**
  - `js/main.js` (`startAct`, the `onEnd(..., 'retry')` path, `saveCheckpoint`);
  - `js/acts/act4.js` and `js/acts/act3.js` (start from a named beat);
  - tests for the resume state (habit log, evidence, whether Ray was called).
- **Cost:** M
- **Keep it ours:** the retry opens on a two-second beat, not a menu: rain on the car roof, or Cooper's
  breathing. A small "saved" icon appears on Mason's phone, so the save itself feels in-world.

### P2. Fail fairly: a reason, a hint, and no repeat scare
- **What:**
  - Every fail already has a reason line. Add a second, more specific hint after the second fail in
    the same spot.
  - From the second fail on, skip the jump scare: cut to black on his hum instead of the closet burst.
- **Evidence:** jump-scare-and-reload on unclear failures is called the worst sin in horror games
  (Scratch Creek), and Carson House is knocked for dying until you find the one right move
  (section 3).
- **Touches:**
  - `js/acts/act3.js` (the bluff fail), `js/acts/act4.js` (all fail kinds);
  - `content/text.js` (new hint lines, hand-written);
  - a small fail counter kept with the checkpoint.
- **Cost:** S
- **Keep it ours:** the hints are Mason thinking back, for example "He tilts his head before he turns.
  Watch for the tilt." This also keeps the 3-jump-scare rule true for players who struggle.

### P3. A "small wrongness" clock for calm stretches
- **What:**
  - In Night 1 before the settled-in gate, and through Day 2, never let more than about 2 or 3 minutes
    pass without a small odd detail or a joke.
  - Examples: a sticky note moved, Cooper staring at the floor, the fish twitching, a TV line.
- **Evidence:**
  - Woodbury and Carson House were punished for long dull stretches.
  - Woodbury critics say the earlier episodes worked through small odd details (section 2, row 1;
    section 3, rows 4 and 5).
- **Touches:**
  - `js/engine/tension.js` (a "calm too long" timer);
  - `js/engine/scarePool.js` / `content/scares.js` (a pool of tiny, non-scary oddities);
  - `js/day2/*.js`.
- **Cost:** S to M
- **Keep it ours:** the oddities come from our house (Dave's notes, Cooper, the fish, the tablet list),
  and none of them is a scare. The fear budget stays where `story-v2.md` puts it.

### P4. Brightness check on the title screen
- **What:** add a brightness slider, plus a one-time "turn it up until you can just see this" picture
  before Night 1.
- **Evidence:** too dark to explore (Stigmatized Property), no brightness control (Exit 8), and dark
  sections that frustrate (Dread Central).
- **Touches:**
  - `js/engine/settings.js` (a new `brightness` key, with a test);
  - `js/menu.js` (the slider and check);
  - `js/render.js` (exposure).
- **Cost:** S
- **Keep it ours:** the test picture is Cooper's silhouette at the end of the dark hall, with "Turn it up
  until you can just see Cooper."

### P5. Make the learning visible
- **What:** when a habit gate fires on Night 2, the player should feel "he knew".
- **Example:** the breaker trips, then Mason thinks "Every light. Every night. Like he was keeping
  count." The crawl notebook lists that same habit.
- **Evidence:** Hello Neighbor's learning AI mostly didn't come through to players or critics (Kotaku,
  Metacritic 38). If players don't notice the learning, it might as well not exist.
- **Touches:**
  - `js/engine/habits.js` (`nightTwoPlan`, which says which gate fired);
  - `js/night2/dread.js` and `js/night2/under.js`;
  - `content/text.js`.
- **Cost:** S
- **Keep it ours:** this is our notebook idea pushed further. No other game here reads your habits back
  to you.

### P6. Mason talks back to silly play
- **What:** hand-written thoughts for goofy or off-path actions. Examples:
  - walking into the treeline at night;
  - flushing five times;
  - petting Cooper for a full minute;
  - opening and closing the fridge over and over;
  - trying the padlocked hatch again and again.
- **Evidence:** F2F players love that the narrator notices when they fall out of the world, and lasagna
  jokes are Home Alone's top reviews (section 2, row 4).
- **Touches:**
  - `content/text.js`;
  - `js/night1/jokes.js` and `js/night1/chores.js`;
  - counters in the habit log (`js/engine/habits.js`).
- **Cost:** S to M
- **Keep it ours:** these are Mason's jokes, not a narrator's. Some feed Wes's notebook later, for
  example "Opened the fridge 9 times. Hungry boy."

### P7. A few more things to pick up and toss
- **What:**
  - One soft, harmless throwable per room: a sock, a pun magnet, a couch cushion, a roll of paper towels.
  - Cooper reacts: he fetches some and refuses others.
- **Evidence:** F2F's most-shared moments are messing with ordinary objects (the lasagna, milk thrown at
  the cashier), and petting the dog is a highlight (section 2).
- **Touches:**
  - `js/decor.js` (turn `ball.throwFrom` into a general throwable);
  - `js/props.js` and `js/cooper.js` (fetching other items);
  - `js/engine/habits.js` (log it).
- **Cost:** M
- **Keep it ours:**
  - Cooper brings back the wrong thing (Dave's slipper).
  - None of it can affect Wes, so it is never a weapon.
  - One item can turn up in the crawl, in Wes's things.

### P8. The pet cam "did that move?" check
- **What:**
  - On Night 2, an object in the pet cam's view (a chair, the remote, Cooper's toy) is in a different
    place between two checks.
  - A pet cam snapshot of both counts as evidence.
- **Evidence:** players love doubting their own memory in I'm on Observation Duty. Carson House's
  cameras were its best system (section 2).
- **Touches:**
  - `js/petcam.js` (snapshots, a before-and-after);
  - `js/engine/evidence.js` (a new item);
  - `js/night2/dread.js`.
- **Cost:** M
- **Keep it ours:**
  - Cooper is in the frame and his head turns before the object moves: the tell is our dog, not a
    rule.
  - It feeds our evidence endings, not a score.
  - One change per night, so it doesn't become a minigame.

### P9. Text replies that matter
- **What:**
  - Pick replies to Ethan and Mom on Night 1 and Day 2: honest ("something's off") or brave ("all
    good").
  - What you told people decides who calls 911 in Act 4, and adds a line in the epilogue.
- **Evidence:**
  - A critic asked for exactly this in Home Alone, to add replay value ([Dread Central](https://www.dreadcentral.com/editorials/483398/ultra-indie-spotlight-sunday-fears-to-fathom-shows-how-terrifying-the-ordinary-can-be-2/)).
  - Scratch Creek players loved free texting.
  - F2F's choices are criticized as not mattering.
- **Touches:**
  - `js/phone.js` and `content/talks.js`;
  - `js/engine/evidence.js`;
  - the sirens in `js/acts/act4.js`.
- **Cost:** M
- **Keep it ours:** it ties into our cast. Ethan's jokes get serious if you told him the truth, and Ray
  "had a report from your friend". Every reply is hand-written.

### P10. The "he could really be there" audit
- **What:**
  - Write a timeline in `docs/story-v2.md` of where Wes is at every beat, and check each appearance is
    physically possible: walking time, the vent, the hatch, the key.
  - Add a test that he never appears in two places faster than he could walk.
- **Evidence:**
  - Players mock a "human" killer who teleports (Dread Neighbor).
  - Ironbark lost fans when it switched to stock horror.
  - A critic says the human prowler is what makes Home Alone scary (sections 2 and 3).
- **Touches:**
  - `docs/story-v2.md`;
  - `js/wes.js`, `js/engine/search.js`, `js/night1/dread.js`;
  - a test in `tests/`.
- **Cost:** S for the timeline, M with the test.
- **Keep it ours:** the payoff is that the epilogue, or a second playthrough, can show his route ("He
  was under the kitchen when you made dinner"). That proves he's real, which is our whole point.

### P11. Remember scares between playthroughs
- **What:** the Director remembers which scare-pool items you saw last run and prefers new ones.
- **Evidence:** The Mortuary Assistant's scare system is its most praised feature because it remembers
  what you've seen ([Bloody Disgusting](https://bloody-disgusting.com/video-games/3726393/the-mortuary-assistant-review-narratively-involving-mechanically-inventive-and-technically-flawed/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mortuary_Assistant)).
- **Touches:**
  - `js/engine/scarePool.js` (accept a "seen before" list, with a test);
  - `js/main.js` (store it in `localStorage`, next to `oakridge.finished`).
- **Cost:** S
- **Keep it ours:** it uses our pool and our Director. Second runs feel like the house has learned you
  too.

### P12. Show what you missed
- **What:**
  - After any evidence ending, the phone's evidence album shows empty "?" slots with a vague hint
    ("Something under the house").
  - Each evidence tier should feel truly different, not just another line.
- **Evidence:**
  - Exit 8 players get frustrated not knowing what they missed.
  - No, I'm not a Human is praised for endings worth chasing but knocked for shallow replays
    (section 3).
- **Touches:**
  - `js/epilogue.js`, `js/engine/evidence.js`;
  - achievements (Phase 6);
  - `js/phone.js` (the photos app).
- **Cost:** S to M
- **Keep it ours:** it's Mason's own camera roll, not a menu of trophies.

### P13. An honest closing card
- **What:**
  - Don't call Oakridge a true story.
  - Write the closing card as "what's real" (phrogging, with checked facts) and "what's made up"
    (Oakridge, Wes, Dave's house).
- **Evidence:**
  - F2F's "based on true events" claim doesn't survive scrutiny ([Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/are-fears-to-fathom-stories-real-story-and-episodes-explained/)).
  - Even its developer can't tell which submissions are true ([Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/input/gaming/mukul-negi-rayll-fears-to-fathom-home-alone-norwood-hitchhike)).
  - `story-v2.md` already says the facts get checked first.
- **Touches:** `content/text.js` (the epilogue lines) and `js/epilogue.js`.
- **Cost:** S
- **Keep it ours:** not F2F's told-by-the-survivor format. It's our own card in the game's VHS style,
  and the honesty makes it scarier: "This house isn't real. Houses like it are."

### P14. Optional microphone mode (off by default)
- **What:**
  - For the hold-still moments (two feet under the bathroom door, the car), the game can listen through
    the mic if the player turns it on.
  - Making noise makes Wes tilt his head toward you first, as a warning, before it can fail you.
- **Evidence:** F2F uses the mic while hiding and it's memorable, but Bluetooth speakers set it off by
  mistake ([ProtonDB](https://www.protondb.com/app/2961530)).
- **Touches:**
  - `js/audio.js` (a mic level meter);
  - `js/engine/settings.js` (an opt-in, with a test);
  - `js/acts/act4.js` (the noise check);
  - `js/menu.js` (a calibration screen).
- **Cost:** M
- **Risk:** we must first test that the browser allows the mic on `file://`. If not, it stays keyboard
  only. Audio is never stored.
- **Keep it ours:** the feedback is Wes's head tilt, our signature, and a warning comes before a fail.
  The calibration screen warns about speakers.

### P15. Readable safe windows in The Search
- **What:** when Wes drops under the house mid-search, make it clearly audible (the hatch creak, then
  footsteps under the floor going away). That gives the player a known 20 to 30 seconds to move.
- **Evidence:** Stay Out of the House players burn out on a killer who is always on them (section 3).
- **Touches:** `js/engine/search.js` (a "below" job), `js/acts/act4.js`, `js/audio.js`.
- **Cost:** S
- **Keep it ours:** it uses the crawlspace, the heart of our story. The danger is still under your feet,
  just not in your face.

### P16. A "stuck?" safety net
- **What:**
  - Add an "I'm stuck" option in the pause menu that moves you to the nearest safe spot.
  - If there's no progress for a few minutes, the to-do hint gets more specific.
- **Evidence:** Hello Neighbor makes players feel stupid, Norwood assumes things about how you'll play,
  and F2F players fall through floors and restart (section 3).
- **Touches:**
  - `js/menu.js` and `js/hud.js`;
  - `js/engine/director.js` (time since last task);
  - the stand points in `content/house.js`.
- **Cost:** S
- **Keep it ours:** the nudge comes as an Ethan text or a Mason thought, not a tutorial box.

### P17. Skip the drives on replay
- **What:** after one finish, the Day 2 drives can be skipped, with a fresh radio line playing over the
  fade.
- **Evidence:**
  - A Norwood player felt half the game was driving down an empty road ([Steam −](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1763050/negativereviews/?browsefilter=toprated&filterLanguage=english)).
  - Minigames that pad the length ([Woodbury Steam +](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2961530/positivereviews/?browsefilter=toprated&filterLanguage=english)).
- **Touches:** `js/day2/trip.js` and `js/places.js`.
- **Cost:** S
- **Keep it ours:** the radio host has a "you again?" line for returning players.

### P18. Writing-pass notes (for Phase 7)
- **What:** two checks for the writing pass.
  - **Keep humor clean of gross-out.** The gag ending stays off-screen.
  - **Answer "why doesn't Mason just leave?"** Give him in-character reasons: Dave is counting on him,
    Ray found nothing, the car is the only way out, and it's dead.
- **Evidence:**
  - Toilet humor drove a fan away (Ironbark).
  - Players hate a hero who acts dumb for the plot (Parasocial).
- **Touches:** `content/text.js` (Day 2 and Night 2 lines).
- **Cost:** S
- **Keep it ours:** it's Mason's voice, and it uses our cast.

### Not a code change: how people find it
- **Pattern:** F2F spread through streamers ([itch.io](https://itch.io/post/808611),
  [Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/subcultures/fears-to-fathom)).
- **Idea:** `dist/` is already one HTML file, which suits a free itch.io page.
- **Rohan's call:** whether and when to publish.
